1. Lower your thermostat by one or two degrees in winter and raise it in summer; each degree saves a few percent on heating and cooling.
2. Replace any remaining incandescent or halogen bulbs with LEDs, which use about a fifth of the electricity.
3. Wash clothes in cold water and only run full loads.
4. Air-dry laundry when you can; the dryer is one of the most power-hungry appliances in the house.
5. Seal gaps around windows, doors, and skirting boards with weatherstripping or caulk to stop draughts.
6. Unplug chargers and put TVs, consoles, and set-top boxes on a switched power strip to cut standby draw.
7. Set your water heater to 60°C (140°F) or slightly lower and insulate the tank and the first metre of pipe.
8. Keep the fridge between 3 and 5°C, leave a gap behind it for airflow, and clean the coils once a year.
9. Cook with lids on pots, match pan size to the burner, and use a microwave or air fryer for small portions instead of the full oven.
10. Close curtains or blinds at night in winter to hold heat in, and during the hottest part of the day in summer to keep it out.
